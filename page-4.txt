THE HOUSE THAT COUNTED • 3     		  	 	
	
2. THE LANDING     		  		 
	
There were thirteen steps to the first floor. Mara counted them twice.     			 	 	
On the landing stood a narrow table and, above it, a mirror with a black	
cloth draped over its frame.     			  		
	
The light switch did nothing. Still, at the far end of the landing, a     		  	 	
yellow glow leaked from beneath a closed door.	
      	     

She knew the room. It had been her father's study, though he had never	
owned this house. As a child, she had once dreamed of standing in that     			 	  
room while a man behind the door counted backward from one	
hundred. She had woken at zero with her father's hand over her mouth.     		 				
The door opened a finger's width. Inside, a desk lamp illuminated a	
ledger. Its pages were filled with names, each followed by a number.      	     Some names had one. Some had hundreds. Her own name appeared
near the bottom.     			  		
MARA VALE — 29.		
Beside it, in fresh ink, someone had written: 30.
