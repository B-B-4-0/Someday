THE HOUSE THAT COUNTED • 7	
     		  	 	
6. THE FIRST NUMBER	
     			  	 
The voice belonged to her brother. It was exactly as she remembered it:	
bright, impatient, almost laughing.     		    	
Mara stepped toward the staircase. The veiled woman caught her wrist.	
Her fingers were cold and surprisingly strong.     			 	  
	
"It knows the shape of what you miss," she warned. "That is not the     		 	  	
same as knowing them."	
     		 				
Mara turned back to the clocks. On the nearest one, the number 1	
appeared. Beneath it, a tiny brass plate bore her father's name.     		 			 
She took the clock from its hook and smashed it against the desk.	
      	     
Every clock stopped. The woman made a sound like a hundred pages	
     	 	    
tearing. The room folded inward. The staircase stretched, then snapped     			  	 
	
back into place.     		 				
Above, the house gave a long, wooden groan. The light on the landing	
went out.     			 	  
	
For the first time in twenty-seven years, Bellweather Lane was     		 				
completely dark.
