THE HOUSE THAT COUNTED • 5	

4. THE ROOM BELOW     		 	  	

The staircase ended in a room that was too large to fit beneath the     		 			 
house. Its ceiling disappeared into blackness. Hundreds of clocks hung	
from wires. None had hands. Each had a small glass window where the     			 	  
numbers should have been.	
    		   	
Behind a desk sat a woman in a grey dress. Her face was hidden by a	
veil of fine white thread. She turned a page in a book although the book     	  	 	 
was empty.	
      		    
“Your father borrowed time,” the woman said. “He thought grief was a	
door. He thought he could open it and bring someone back.”     	  	 	 
	
Mara's throat tightened. “My brother?”      	    	
	
“The house does not return what it takes. It returns what it has learned     	      
to imitate.”	
     	 	 	 	
The clocks began to tick. One by one, their glass windows filled with      		 	  	
numbers. Every clock showed 29.      		 	  
	
The woman lifted her veiled head. “It has nearly finished counting     	 	    
you.”
